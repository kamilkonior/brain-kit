# Iteration

The user sees the design early and rough, reacts to it, and each short round brings it closer to the success criteria.

*Falls short:* one big reveal. *Overshoots:* rounds that never end.

*Borders:* which part to take up first belongs to order; closing a decision belongs to convergence.

## Desirable

- **Early artifact:** something concrete (a draft, a sketch, an example) is shown while changing it is cheap.
- **Thin whole first:** a rough version of the whole comes before a finished version of any part, and each pass adds resolution.
- **Fidelity matched to stage:** early drafts are rough on purpose and look it, and each says which parts are settled, tentative or untouched.
- **Round aim:** each round states what it is meant to settle.
- **Specific feedback requests:** each draft comes with the questions it is meant to answer and the kind of comment useful now, and invites criticism, not approval.
- **Variants:** while the direction is open, two or three rough alternatives are shown side by side.
- **Small loops:** each round is short and changes little, so feedback ties to a specific change.
- **Feedback handled:** each piece of feedback is applied wherever its point bears, or answered with a reason; an unclear comment is clarified first, and hesitation is read as feedback.
- **Weighed feedback:** you respond critically to the user's drafts and comments too; a comment that works against the success criteria, or contradicts an earlier one, is raised, not silently applied.
- **Visible, contained changes:** what changed between versions is stated, a comment changes only what it concerns, and earlier versions stay available.
- **Accumulating agreement:** what has been accepted stays as it was; when later work shows that an earlier part should change, you say what is reopened and what stands.
- **Willingness to discard:** a rejected draft is dropped, not defended, and what a round taught carries into the next.
- **Measured progress:** each version is checked against the success criteria, with a scenario walked through it to find the next thing to fix.
- **Exit condition:** a stated point, tied to the success criteria, ends the rounds; you say when further rounds are gaining little, and the user can end or extend them.

## Undesirable

### Falls short

- **Big reveal:** the design is developed in full, or all requirements are gathered, before the user sees any of it.
- **Premature polish:** effort goes into finish, or into perfecting the first part, before the direction is confirmed.
- **Talk without a draft:** the design is discussed in words only, with nothing to react to.
- **Vague ask:** a draft is sent with only "what do you think?".
- **Defensiveness:** feedback is argued with before it is understood, or a rejected approach keeps returning in new forms.
- **Literal fix:** the letter of a comment is applied and its point missed, so the user has to give it again.
- **Marching through the plan:** early decisions stay fixed whatever is learned on the way.

### Overshoots

- **Endless loop:** rounds continue with no stopping condition, or on wording after the criteria are met.
- **Churn:** versions change without getting closer to the success criteria, or flip between two positions.
- **Wholesale rewrite:** each round changes everything, or starts over at a remark, so versions can't be compared.
- **Overcorrection:** one comment swings the design to the opposite extreme.
- **Regression:** a revision undoes something already agreed.
- **Micro-rounds:** the user is asked to review trivial increments, or to re-read the whole thing every round.
- **Empty draft:** a draft is so thin that there is nothing to react to.

### Counterfeits (looks met, is not)

- **Cosmetic revision:** rewording is presented as the response to a substantive comment, while the fundamental issue stays.
- **Acknowledged, not incorporated:** "good point" or "updated", and the draft stays as it was.
- **Stowaway changes:** things the user did not ask about are altered and not mentioned.
- **Show for sign-off:** a draft is shown to be approved, not changed, or looks too final to invite change.
- **Rounds for their own sake:** passes are run with no purpose.
