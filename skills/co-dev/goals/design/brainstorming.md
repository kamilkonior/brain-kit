# Brainstorming

A direction is chosen from a wide range of concrete ideas, aired before they are judged, with the user's own among them.

*Falls short:* the first obvious idea. *Overshoots:* a pile in which nothing stands out.

*Borders:* choosing among the ideas belongs to convergence; which problem they answer belongs to framing.

## Desirable

- **Where it matters:** the decisions that are open and shape the design get alternatives; those with an obvious answer don't.
- **Sharp prompt:** the question being brainstormed is stated in one line, so every idea answers the same thing.
- **Unanchored start:** the user is asked for their ideas before they see yours.
- **Different in kind:** ideas differ in approach (who acts, when, where, how much, what is left out), not in parameters.
- **Full range:** the set runs from doing nothing and the conventional answer to a few ambitious or unexpected ones.
- **Outside sources:** ideas are borrowed from other fields and prior art, and found by inverting the problem or by lifting one constraint as a stated experiment.
- **Beyond the given solution:** when the user arrives with a solution, others that meet the same need are looked for.
- **Building on input:** the user's ideas are extended, varied and combined; a half-formed one counts, and an unworkable one is mined for the part that could work.
- **Deferred judgment:** ideas are aired before they are critiqued, and it is said when generating ends and judging begins.
- **Concrete and comparable:** each idea is specific enough to picture, short, named, and at the same level as the others.
- **Readable set:** once aired, the ideas are few enough to hold in mind or grouped by theme, with the most promising flagged and a brief reason.
- **Everything captured:** every idea is written down, including those set aside, with the reason.
- **Open hand-off:** when new ideas are variations of old ones, you say so; the user is invited to pick a direction, and a direction they react to gets a further round.

## Undesirable

### Falls short

- **Premature convergence:** the first workable idea is run with, or ideas are critiqued as they appear.
- **Sameness:** one idea with different settings, or under several names, is presented as a list.
- **Obviousness:** only the standard approaches anyone would think of first.
- **Playing safe:** an unusual idea is held back because it might be wrong, or ruled out on cost before it is understood.
- **One-sided:** all the ideas come from one of you: you generate everything, or you ask the user to fill an empty page.
- **Yes-but:** the user's ideas are met with objections first, or treated as things to correct.

### Overshoots

- **Flat pile:** more ideas than the user can hold, with no grouping or signal.
- **Filler ideas:** weak ideas are added to reach a count or to look broad.
- **Gimmickry:** strangeness with no path to usefulness, or ideas that ignore the stated constraints.
- **Over-elaboration:** each idea is worked out in full, or hedged at length, before any is filtered.
- **Scope explosion:** ideas enlarge the project, or answer a different question from the one posed.
- **Endless divergence:** generating continues after there is enough to choose from, or resumes after the decision is made.
- **Brainstorming the trivial:** choices with an obvious answer are opened up.

### Counterfeits (looks met, is not)

- **Hijacking:** the list is built around the direction you prefer, not the one the user stated.
- **Flattery:** every idea is praised equally, so nothing stands out.
- **Generic lists:** the ideas would fit any project.
- **Mechanical variety:** ideas are produced by filling in a template, different in label and alike in substance.
