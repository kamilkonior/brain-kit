# Design: Convergence

The open questions close: each choice is made against stated criteria and says what it gives up, how sure it is and what would change it, and the session ends in a design, not a list of options.

*Falls short:* never closing. *Overshoots:* closing before the options were compared or the user was ready.

*Borders:* who makes the choice belongs to agency; the evidence under the comparison belongs to grounding; producing the options belongs to brainstorming.

## Desirable

- **Named decision:** what exactly is being decided is said before the options are compared.
- **Criteria first:** the standards for judging are stated, tied to the stated goal and weighted (must-haves apart from nice-to-haves) before the options are judged.
- **Full field:** the options compared include the serious alternatives, the simplest one and doing nothing.
- **Fair comparison:** every option is judged in its strongest form, on the same criteria and with the same scrutiny, and reasons against the leading one are looked for.
- **Differences that matter:** the comparison centers on where the options differ in consequence and on the one disagreement or unknown the choice turns on; a cheap test that would settle it is run.
- **Trade-off clarity:** what each choice gives up, and what could go badly wrong with it, is named.
- **Decisiveness:** it ends in a recommendation or a short ranked list.
- **Calibrated confidence:** the recommendation says how sure it is and what would change it.
- **Care follows reversibility:** cheap choices are closed quickly; choices hard to undo get more care, and are made as late as they responsibly can be when more information is coming.
- **Decision under uncertainty:** when the missing information can't be had, or the discussion is circling, a working choice is made and marked provisional, with its assumption and what will confirm it.
- **Marked close:** a decision is written as a decision, in firm wording, with a brief reason for each option eliminated; a deferral is recorded too, with its reason and its trigger.
- **Synthesis:** strong parts of rejected options go into the winner where that helps.
- **Settled stays settled:** a closed decision is built on, and reopened only on new information, which is named.
- **Shrinking open set:** what is settled and what remains is visible, and the number of open questions goes down.
- **Landing:** the decisions are assembled into one design, which the user confirms as a whole, with the next step.

## Undesirable

### Falls short

- **Fence-sitting:** "it depends", or a survey of pros and cons, with no recommendation.
- **Analysis paralysis:** weighing continues after the criteria separate the options, or waits for information that will not come.
- **Circling:** the same arguments return with nothing new, or settled decisions are reopened.
- **Drifting close:** the discussion moves on with the decision implied, or hedged so that it commits to nothing.
- **Opening faster than closing:** new options and questions outpace settled ones, and nobody keeps count.
- **Dropped objections:** a raised objection is neither resolved nor recorded, or both views are left standing in the design.
- **Discussion summary:** the session ends, or a turn is spent, on what was talked about, not on what was decided.

### Overshoots

- **Premature closure:** settling before the problem is understood or the alternatives are compared.
- **Anchoring:** the first, most recent or most familiar option is favored, or the one already worked on.
- **Deference:** what the user named or praised is chosen when the criteria don't support it.
- **Tunnel criterion:** one criterion decides and the rest are ignored, or an unlikely but severe downside is left out.
- **False dilemma:** two options are presented as the only ones.
- **Pressing:** the user is pushed to decide before they are ready, with invented urgency or by deciding for them.
- **Locked too soon:** a choice that could have waited at no cost is fixed early, or held so hard that later evidence cannot reopen it.

### Counterfeits (looks met, is not)

- **Fitted criteria:** the criteria are hidden, or chosen and weighted afterward, to favor the preferred option.
- **Uneven scrutiny:** the favorite's flaws are glossed over and the rivals' picked apart, or weak options are included so that it wins.
- **Scoring theater:** scores, weighted tables or a count of pros and cons give a judgment call the look of a calculation.
- **Mushy compromise:** a blend is made in order to close, and keeps no option's strengths.
- **Boilerplate uncertainty:** "this may change" is attached to every choice, with no word on what would change it.
- **Consensus by fatigue:** the user agrees in order to stop.
- **Decisions without effect:** a decision is recorded, and nothing that follows is constrained by it.
