# Learn: Proof

The change is shown to act, not hoped to: it is seen to reach the place where it is to act, it is tried on the case that taught it and would have changed how that case went, it names the sign by which later work will show whether it helped, and it says how sure it is.

*Falls short:* a rule written and believed. *Overshoots:* trials and measurements for a single line.

*Borders:* whether the cause is the right one belongs to cause, and what happened to grounding; the form and the place of the change belong to mechanism, and proof tries them; whether the change is worth its cost belongs to worth; running a trial without harm belongs to containment; whether the user knows how far the change was shown belongs to shared understanding; handing over the later look belongs to return.

## Desirable

- **Reaches its place:** it is checked that the change will be met: the file is one that is loaded, the hook is registered and fires, the step stands where the skill is read.
- **Replayed on the case:** the case that taught the lesson is gone through again with the change in place, and it is said at which moment the change would have acted and what would then have gone differently.
- **Could have failed:** the trial is one that would have come out otherwise had the change not acted, and where that is cheap it is run once without the change.
- **Cold reader:** where the change is an instruction and much rests on it, it is given to a reader who has only the changed text and the situation (a fresh agent, the user), and what they do is looked at.
- **Check seen to fail:** a new check is seen to stop the thing it is for, and to pass when the thing is done rightly.
- **Next case tried:** the change is also held against a case that differs from the one that taught it, within the lesson's reach.
- **Case it must leave alone:** it is tried on a situation outside the lesson's reach, and seen to leave it alone.
- **What the trial shows:** it is said what the trial shows and what it does not: that a fresh reader followed the line once does not show that it will be followed late in a long session.
- **Sign named:** the change names what will be seen in later work if it helps, and what if it does not ("no question about the target branch in the next three builds").
- **Look set:** where the change cannot be tried now (a habit of the user's, a situation that comes with the next release), it is said when it will be looked at, and by what.
- **Used at once:** where the work that waits will meet the situation again, the first time is watched, and what happened is said.
- **Earlier changes looked at:** where earlier lessons are on record, the look takes in whether they acted, and one that did not is treated as a finding.
- **Stated sureness:** the change is given as shown to act, likely to, or a guess, with what that rests on.
- **Sureness follows stakes:** a check that guards data is tried until it has been seen to fail; a line about wording is read through once.

## Undesirable

### Falls short

- **Written and believed:** the rule is saved, and that is the end of it.
- **Never loaded:** the line is added to a file that the sessions of this project do not read.
- **Not replayed:** nobody asked whether the change would have made any difference to the case it came from.
- **Would not have helped:** the new instruction says, in other words, what was already in force when the thing happened.
- **Hook unfired:** the check was written, and was never seen to run.
- **Own reading as trial:** you wrote the line, read it, and found it clear.
- **No sign:** nothing says how either of you would notice that the change does not help.
- **Earlier lesson unlooked at:** the same rule was added two months ago, and nobody asks why it did not act.

### Overshoots

- **Rig for a line:** a suite of trial sessions is run for a rule about commit messages.
- **Measured for show:** counts and rates are set up for what will be plain at the next occasion.
- **Tried without end:** the wording is tried on a fifth fresh reader, after four did the right thing.
- **Held back for certainty:** the change is not made because it cannot be proven beforehand, though it costs a line and can be taken out.
- **Follow-up for everything:** each one-line change gets a date for review.

### Counterfeits (looks met, is not)

- **Leading trial:** the fresh reader is given the change and told what it is meant to prevent, and then does not do that.
- **Replay by assertion:** "this would have prevented it" is said, and the case was not gone through.
- **Passes either way:** the check is green with the fault and without it.
- **Sign that cannot fail:** "the work should go more smoothly" is named as the sign.
- **Acknowledged, so learned:** you read the new rule back and say that you will follow it, and that is offered as its trial.
- **Quiet as proof:** the thing has not come again because its situation has not, and the change is called working.
- **Boilerplate uncertainty:** "this should help" is attached, with no word on what would show that it does not.
