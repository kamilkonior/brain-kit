# Learn: Mechanism

The lesson is turned into a change that acts without anyone remembering it: it is put where the next piece of work will meet it at the moment it applies, in a form strong enough to hold (made automatic or impossible where a slip would be costly, a check where it can be checked, an instruction where judgment is needed, and never an intention alone), and worded so that a reader who was not here does what was meant.

*Falls short:* a resolution, or a note that nothing will ever read. *Overshoots:* gates and automation where a sentence would hold.

*Borders:* in which situations the lesson holds belongs to reach; whether the change then does act belongs to proof; what it costs later work belongs to worth; how it sits with what is already there belongs to coherence; whether it may be put at that level belongs to agency and to containment; telling the user where it is belongs to return.

## Desirable

- **A carrier named:** each lesson that is kept gets a carrier that outlasts the session (a line in the instructions read at the start of a session, a step or a trait in a skill, a check or a hook, a script, a template, a written agreement), and none is left in the conversation.
- **Met at the moment:** the carrier is one the work passes when the lesson applies: the instruction file for what holds all the time, the skill for what holds in that kind of work, the plan's template for what holds when a plan is made, the hook for the command.
- **Loaded, not only stored:** it is known that the place is read when it is needed: by you at the start of the session or of the task, by the user when they do the thing.
- **Form by what a slip costs:** what must not happen even once is made impossible or stopped by a check; what should usually happen is an instruction.
- **Form by what can be told:** what a program can tell (a command, a file touched, a test not run) is given to a program; what needs judgment is given to an instruction.
- **Cause removed first:** where the cause itself can be removed (the misleading script renamed, the missing fact written where it is looked for), that is preferred to a rule about living with it.
- **Says what to do:** an instruction names the situation and what to do in it, not only what to avoid.
- **Reason with it:** it carries its reason in a clause, so that it can be applied with judgment.
- **Case with it:** where the words could be read two ways, a short case from the work shows what is meant.
- **Cold wording:** it has no "as we saw" and no name coined in the session, and reads rightly to one who has only the file.
- **Can be told when followed:** it is worded so that it can be seen afterward whether it was followed.
- **The user's side carried too:** a change on the user's side gets a carrier that does not rest on their memory either, where they want one: a template for the request, a question you are instructed to ask at the start, a line in a checklist they already use.
- **Agreement written:** a change in who decides what, or in where the work pauses, is written where both of you will meet it, in the words that were agreed.
- **Skill changed at its source:** where the lesson is that a skill led the wrong way, the change is made to that skill (its step, its trait), not worked around in another file.
- **Existing carrier repaired:** where a rule existed and did not act, it is moved, sharpened or given a stronger form, and no second one is added.
- **Lightest that holds:** of the forms that would hold, the lightest is taken.
- **Proportion:** a fact about the project is a line in the project's notes.

## Undesirable

### Falls short

- **Resolution:** "I'll check the branch from now on" is the change, and the next session will not know that it was said.
- **Lesson in the chat:** the lesson was agreed in the conversation, and is written nowhere.
- **Dead file:** the lesson is written into a notes file that no session loads and nobody opens.
- **Wrong moment:** the rule about releases stands deep in a file read once at the start, and is out of view by the time a release is made.
- **Sentence for a must:** what must never happen is left to one sentence among sixty.
- **Only a don't:** the rule says what not to do, and nothing says how the situation is to be met.
- **Session words:** the line reads "use the second approach for imports", and only the session knows which that was.
- **The user's side left to memory:** "you'll name the target branch in future" is the whole change on the user's side.
- **Workaround beside the skill:** the skill still says the thing that misled, and a line elsewhere says to ignore it.

### Overshoots

- **Gate for a preference:** a hook blocks every commit, for a matter of style that a line would carry.
- **Automation for once:** a script is written for a situation that comes twice a year.
- **Everything enforced:** each lesson becomes a check, and the work waits on checks.
- **New system:** a store of lessons, a tagging scheme and a review cycle are set up to hold three lines.
- **New skill:** a skill is written for what is one sentence in an existing one.
- **Belt and braces:** the same lesson is put into the instructions, a skill, a hook and a memory.

### Counterfeits (looks met, is not)

- **Emphasis as strength:** the rule is written in capitals, with "never" and "critical", and is still a sentence that may not be read.
- **"Noted":** "noted for next time" is said, and nothing was written.
- **Stored, not met:** the file exists and the rule is in it, and nothing brings it into view at the moment it applies.
- **Check that cannot fail:** the new check passes whether or not the thing happened.
- **Wish as rule:** "write clearer plans" is filed as the change.
- **Template nobody uses:** a form for requests is made, and the user never asked for one and will not open it.
