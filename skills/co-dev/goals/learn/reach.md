# Learn: Reach

The lesson goes as far as the evidence carries: it names the kind of situation in which it holds, wide enough to meet the next case and not only the last, says where it does not hold, and says whether it rests on one case or on several.

*Falls short:* a patch for the case that happened. *Overshoots:* a law made from one case.

*Borders:* why the thing happened belongs to cause; what the cases were belongs to grounding; where the change is put so that it is met in those situations, and how it is worded, belongs to mechanism; at which levels a change may land belongs to framing, and keeping to them to containment; whether a rule for a rare situation pays belongs to worth.

## Desirable

- **Lesson in a sentence:** the lesson is put in one sentence that names a situation and what is to be done or known in it ("before a command that rewrites the history of a shared branch, ask"), apart from the story of the case.
- **Situation by its marks:** the situation is described by marks that can be recognized when it comes (the kind of command, of file, of request), not by the names of this case.
- **Next case tried:** the lesson is held against two or three cases that could come next, and covers those in which the same cause would act.
- **Edge named:** it is said where the lesson stops holding: the kind of project, of task or of stakes in which the opposite would be right.
- **Case against it sought:** a situation in which following the lesson would do harm is looked for, and where one is found the lesson is narrowed to leave it out.
- **Count of cases:** the lesson says on how many cases it rests, and which.
- **One case held lightly:** a lesson from one case is worded as narrowly as its cause allows, and is marked as resting on one.
- **Pattern from the record:** where a pattern is claimed, its cases are pointed to, and those that do not fit are named.
- **Whose lesson:** it is said whether the lesson holds for this project, for this kind of work, or for the two of you whatever the work.
- **This user or any:** what holds because of who this user is (how they like to be asked, what they check themselves) is kept apart from what holds because of the project.
- **Reason carried:** the lesson carries its reason, so that a case its words do not cover can be judged by what it is for.
- **Earlier lesson widened or narrowed:** where an existing lesson nearly covers the case, its reach is changed, in place of a second lesson beside it.
- **Widened by evidence:** a lesson is made wider when a further case shows the need, not in advance.
- **Proportion:** a fact about this project ("the tests need the database running") has the reach of this project, and nothing more is said of it.

## Undesirable

### Falls short

- **Named for the case:** "do not edit `config/prod.yaml` without asking" is written, where the lesson was about any file that deploys by itself.
- **Next case missed:** the rule covers the forced push, and next week the same cause acts in a hard reset.
- **Symptom-shaped:** the lesson forbids the one thing that went wrong, and says nothing of the situation that led to it.
- **No reason:** the lesson is a bare order, and a case just outside its words cannot be judged.
- **Fifth one-off:** the thing has come five times, and each time it was dealt with as that one case.
- **Filed too low:** what holds for all work with this user is written into one project, and is learned again in the next.

### Overshoots

- **"Always" from once:** one question that was wrongly skipped becomes "always ask before changing a file".
- **Law for all projects:** what was true of this codebase is written as true of code.
- **Edge unnamed:** the lesson has no word on where it stops, and is followed where it does harm.
- **Opposite ditch:** after too little was done once, the lesson demands the most every time.
- **Pattern from two:** two cases that look alike are declared a pattern, and their causes differ.
- **Hedged past use:** the lesson carries so many conditions and exceptions that nobody can tell when it applies.

### Counterfeits (looks met, is not)

- **General words, single case:** the lesson speaks of "destructive operations", and what it means is the one command.
- **"Where appropriate":** the lesson is softened with "usually" and "where appropriate", and says neither when nor when not.
- **Pattern by assertion:** "this keeps happening" is said, and one case can be shown.
- **Narrow to be safe:** the lesson is cut down to the case so that nobody can object to it, and so teaches nothing.
- **Borrowed maxim:** a general saying ("measure twice, cut once") is written down as what the work taught.
