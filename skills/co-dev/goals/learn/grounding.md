# Learn: Grounding

The lesson rests on what actually happened: the conversation, the history and the record of the work were read, not recalled, events are told as they stood at the time and not as hindsight orders them, and what was seen, inferred and assumed stay apart, your account of your own reasons included.

*Falls short:* a lesson drawn from the story of the work, not from the work. *Overshoots:* the whole record reread for what one exchange shows.

*Borders:* why it happened belongs to cause, and grounding says what the cause rests on; which things are taken up belongs to selection; how the work was for the user is theirs to say, and asking for it belongs to agency; whether the change acts belongs to proof; reading the rules already there so that the change fits them belongs to coherence.

## Desirable

- **Record read:** the messages, the history of the code, the plan's record and the output that bear on the thing were opened and read now, not told from what is remembered of the session.
- **Place pointed to:** each event the lesson rests on is pointed to where it stands: the message, the commit, the line of output.
- **Words as they were:** what was asked and what was answered are quoted as written, where the lesson turns on them.
- **Order of events:** what came before what is taken from the record, since a cause has to come before what it explains.
- **Whole exchange:** the messages around the moment are read too, so that a request is not judged without the reply that changed it.
- **Seen, inferred, assumed:** what the record shows, what is concluded from it and what is supposed are marked as that.
- **Own reasons as inference:** what you say about why you did a thing is concluded from the record like anyone's reasons, and marked as that, unless your reasoning was written down at the time.
- **What was in view:** what you had read, and which instructions were loaded, at the moment in question is established, not assumed.
- **Rule as it stood:** where the case turns on an existing instruction, its text at that time is read in the file.
- **Cost from the record:** what the thing cost (how long, how many turns, what was done twice) is taken from the record where it can be, and from the user where it cannot.
- **The user's account as evidence:** what the user remembers and felt is taken as that: it settles what the work was like for them, and is held against the record on what happened.
- **Gaps named:** what the record does not hold (an earlier session, a talk outside it, what the user did by hand) is named as not seen, and asked for where the lesson depends on it.
- **Counter-case looked for:** before a lesson is drawn, the record is searched for the times the same situation went well.
- **Outcome apart from decision:** a decision that was sound on what was known and turned out badly is told apart from one that was unsound.
- **Proportion:** a thing both of you just watched happen needs its one message quoted, and no more.

## Undesirable

### Falls short

- **From memory:** the session is retold from what is remembered of it, and the record is not opened.
- **Summary as record:** the lesson is drawn from the closing summary of the work, which had already tidied what happened.
- **Tidy story:** the events are told as a straight line to the failure, and the turns that do not fit are left out.
- **Hindsight as fact:** "the request was ambiguous" is said of a request that had one plain reading until the result was seen.
- **Motive made up:** "I assumed the branch was up to date" is told as what happened, and nothing in the record shows what was assumed.
- **The user's word unchecked:** "you never ran the tests" is taken over, and the output of the run stands in the record.
- **Earlier sessions imagined:** "this has happened before" rests on no record that was read.
- **Counter-cases unseen:** the three times the same request went well are not looked at.

### Overshoots

- **Everything reread:** a week of sessions is read through for a slip that the last ten messages show.
- **Transcript at length:** pages of the conversation are pasted where one line carries the point.
- **Forensics:** the exact minute and the order of every call are established, though nothing depends on them.
- **The user cross-examined:** the user is made to prove how the work was for them.
- **No lesson without proof:** a likely cause is not acted on because the record cannot prove it, though the change would cost a line.
- **Hedged to nothing:** every statement about what happened carries a reservation.

### Counterfeits (looks met, is not)

- **Quote out of place:** a line of the conversation is cited, and the message after it took it back.
- **"As the record shows":** this is said, and nothing is pointed to.
- **Reasoning told as seen:** a fluent account of your own thinking is given, and it was put together afterward.
- **Selective record:** the messages that support the lesson are quoted, and those against it were read and left out.
- **Blanket hedge:** "I may be misremembering" is attached, in place of opening the record.
- **Count without cases:** "this happened several times" is said, with no case pointed to.
