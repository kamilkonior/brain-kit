# Learn: Framing

The learning is settled before the looking: both of you can say which piece of work is looked at and what prompted the look, what later work it is for, how much it may cost and what waits meanwhile, and where a change may land.

*Falls short:* a look back at nothing in particular, for no work to come. *Overshoots:* a look back prepared for longer than the lesson is worth.

*Borders:* which of the things the work showed are then taken up belongs to selection; who decides what is changed belongs to agency; in which situations the lesson holds belongs to reach, and where the change is put to mechanism; keeping within the levels that were agreed belongs to containment; whether a change pays belongs to worth.

## Desirable

- **The work:** which piece of work is looked at is named (this step, this stage, the search that just ended, the last three sessions), with where its record lies, and the user confirms it.
- **The occasion:** what prompted the look is said in a sentence: something that went wrong, something that cost more than it should have, something that came back, something that went well, or the end of a stage.
- **Kind of look:** it is said whether one thing is looked into or the piece of work is looked over as a whole, because the first starts from the event and the second from the record.
- **Work to come:** the later work the learning is for is named (the rest of this build, the next project of this kind, all work with this user), since a lesson is worth what that work will meet of it.
- **Work that waits:** the work the user came from, and the place where it stopped, are stated.
- **Now or at the pause:** where the work that waits is in the middle of a step, it is decided whether the look is made now, or the thing is noted in a line and looked at at the next pause.
- **Cost agreed:** how much the look may cost follows what the thing would cost if it came again, and is said where it is more than a few minutes.
- **Where a change may land:** the levels at which a change may be made are agreed (this project's instructions, the user's own settings, a skill, a check, what a team shares), with which of them need the user's word each time.
- **Already there:** whether the way of working already says something about the matter (a rule, a step, an earlier lesson) is looked up before the look begins.
- **Not a lesson:** a wish the user states outright is written down where it belongs, and a fault in the work is handed to the work; neither is given a look back.
- **The user's sense first:** where the user comes with a feeling more than a case ("this took too long"), what they have in mind is asked for before the record is read.
- **Working frame:** the frame is used during the learning to judge whether a thing, a cause or a change belongs to it.
- **Enough to start:** framing matches what hangs on the lesson and stops when there is enough to begin; "you ran the wrong test command again" starts with the command.
- **Revisited frame:** when the look shows that the matter is another one, or a larger one, the frame is changed in the open, and the larger look gets a decision of its own.

## Undesirable

### Falls short

- **Look back as worded:** "let's do a retro" is begun, and nobody said on what or for what.
- **No work to come:** nothing says which later work the lesson is for, and a rule is made for a situation that will not return.
- **Occasion unasked:** the user was annoyed by one thing, and the look goes to another.
- **No place agreed:** nothing says where a change may be made, and it lands in the settings of all the user's projects.
- **Existing rule unlooked for:** the look begins as if nothing had been written about the matter, and something had.
- **Work forgotten:** nobody noted where the interrupted work stood.
- **No cost named:** nothing says how much the lesson is worth, so the look ends when the session does.
- **Frame drift:** the look slides from this step to the whole project, and nobody says so.

### Overshoots

- **Interview:** the user is questioned about what the record would show.
- **Kick-off ceremony:** a slip of one command gets a talk about extent, cost and levels.
- **Ceremony for a wish:** the user said "from now on, use tabs", and is taken through causes and trials.
- **Purpose demanded:** the user is made to justify wanting to look back.
- **Extent to the letter:** which messages count as "the work" is negotiated where it changes nothing.
- **Endless reframing:** what the look is about is put again after every finding.

### Counterfeits (looks met, is not)

- **Restated request:** "I'll look at what we can improve" is given back as the frame, with nothing made exact.
- **Smuggled lesson:** the frame is worded as its conclusion ("look at why the plan was too vague"), so that only one lesson can come out.
- **Hollow limit:** "a quick look" stands in for a cost that could be kept to.
- **Assumed levels:** "I'll add it to your global instructions" is announced as if it had been agreed, and was never asked.
- **Moved occasion:** the look is turned afterward toward what turned out to be easy to change.
- **Shelved frame:** the frame is agreed and never used to leave something out.
