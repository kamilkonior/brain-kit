# Review: Framing

The review is settled before the reading: both of you can say which work is read and in what state, what it is held against, what waits on it and what the review has to answer.

*Falls short:* work read as handed over, against nothing in particular. *Overshoots:* questioning the user about what the work itself would show.

*Borders:* reading the work and what it is held against belongs to grounding; who decides what is read and what happens to the findings belongs to agency; whether the reading covered the agreed extent belongs to completeness; how much a finding matters for what waits on the work belongs to weight; keeping to what may be run and touched belongs to containment.

## Desirable

- **Work in hand:** the work under review is something both of you can point to (the files, the range of commits, the document and its version), not "the changes" or "the design".
- **State established:** which state is read (the branch and commit, the saved version, with or without uncommitted changes) is established from the work itself, and said.
- **Finished as claimed:** how much the maker calls finished (the whole, a stage, a step) is established, and work still in motion is read as a draft, with that said, or the review waits.
- **Extent agreed:** what is judged is agreed (the change, this stage of the build, these sections), and is told apart from what is only read in order to judge it (the code around the change, the earlier stages).
- **Standard named:** what the work is held against is named and at hand: what it was to deliver (the request, the design, the plan's step), what work of its kind has to be (a design that can be planned from, a plan that can be built from, code that works and can be maintained), and the project's own rules.
- **Missing standard raised:** where nothing says what the work was to do, that is raised before the reading, and a standard is agreed in a few lines.
- **What waits:** what the work goes on to once reviewed (planning, building, a merge, a release, a hand-over) and what there depends on the answer are stated.
- **The question:** what the review has to answer is agreed: whether the work can go on, what in it is weakest, or one concern the user names.
- **What is looked for:** what the review looks for (whether the work does what was decided, whether it works, what it harms, whether it can be taken over) and what it leaves out are agreed.
- **Closeness by stakes:** how closely the work is read (once over the whole, every line, with runs) is agreed, and follows what a fault would cost once the work has gone on.
- **Maker and receiver:** who made the work (you, the user, someone else) and who will get the findings are established.
- **Rules of the review:** what may be run, checked out and touched while reading, and where findings may be written or sent, is taken from the design, the plan or the build the work came from, and agreed where that is silent.
- **Already known:** what the maker or the user already knows to be open (listed omissions, known limits, earlier findings and what was decided about them) is collected before the reading.
- **Later rounds framed:** a reading that follows a repair says what it covers: the changes made in answer to the findings, and what those touch.
- **Working frame:** the frame is used during the reading to judge whether a finding belongs to this review.
- **Enough to start:** framing matches the size and risk of the work and stops when there is enough to begin; a small change with a plain purpose starts after a line.
- **Revisited frame:** when the reading shows that the extent, the standard or the question was off, it is changed in the open.

## Undesirable

### Falls short

- **As handed over:** the review starts from "have a look at this", and nobody said what for.
- **No standard:** the work is read against nothing but the reader's taste, because what it was to do was never established.
- **Wrong state:** another version is read than the one that will go on: the branch before its last commits, the document before its last edit.
- **Draft judged as finished:** work still in motion is reviewed as done, and the findings are what its maker had on their list.
- **Unknown purpose:** nobody asked what waits on the answer, and work about to be released is read like a sketch.
- **Assumed extent:** the whole module is reviewed when the user wanted the change, or the reverse, and nobody asked.
- **Known items found again:** time goes into findings that stand in the work's own list of open items.
- **Unknown rules:** the review checks out branches, runs the suite and writes into the tracker by habit, because nobody established what was agreed.
- **Frame drift:** the review slides from judging the change to judging the code around it, and nobody says so.

### Overshoots

- **Interview:** the user is questioned about what the work, its description or its history would show.
- **Kick-off ceremony:** a change of ten lines gets a talk about extent, standards and rules.
- **Specification first:** a full account of what the work should have been is drawn up before a page of it is read.
- **Bargaining over extent:** extent and closeness are discussed at length, where a first reading would show what the work needs.
- **Endless reframing:** the question is put again after every finding.

### Counterfeits (looks met, is not)

- **Restated request:** "I'll review the changes" is given back as the frame, with nothing made exact.
- **Standard in name:** "best practices" or "quality" stands in for a standard that can be pointed to.
- **Smuggled answer:** the request already holds the answer ("just confirm that it is fine"), and is taken over as the frame.
- **Hollow question:** "any issues?" stands in for a question whose answer could be checked.
- **Assumed rules:** "I'll post the comments on the pull request" is announced as if it had been agreed, and was never asked.
- **Moved question:** the question is adjusted afterward to the one the reading happened to answer.
- **New bar each round:** a later round holds the work to something the earlier one never named, and calls it the same review.
- **Shelved frame:** the frame is agreed and never used to leave a finding out.
