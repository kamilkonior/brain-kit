# Review: Return

The findings go back to the work they concern: they are written where that work keeps its record, each in a form that can be acted on without the review session, the user's decision on each is recorded with it, and the user is brought back to where they came from.

*Falls short:* findings that stay in the conversation. *Overshoots:* a report nobody asked for, or the repairs made on the way out.

*Borders:* who decides what is done about a finding belongs to agency; how much a finding matters belongs to weight; whether the user has the same picture of the findings belongs to shared understanding; removing what the review added belongs to containment; how long the report in the conversation is belongs to economy; the repair itself belongs to the work returned to.

## Desirable

- **Findings written:** each finding is written in a few lines: its kind and grade, the place, the case and what the work then does, what that is measured against, what it would cost, and how it was shown and how sure it is.
- **Where the work keeps it:** the findings are written where the work keeps its record (the plan's record, the design's open items, the pull request, the issue), and where there is no such place, where the user says.
- **Acted on cold:** each finding can be understood and acted on by someone who has the work and not the session: the maker, a later builder, the user next week.
- **What would settle it:** a finding says what would count as settled (the case that must then work, the decision that must be made), and may name a way there without prescribing it.
- **Where it goes:** each finding says where its fault belongs (in this work, in the plan or the design it follows, in the request itself) and so what it calls for: a repair within the work, a change to the plan, a decision of the design to reopen, a question to someone outside, or nothing.
- **Decision recorded:** what the user decided about each finding (repair it, leave it, defer it, take it to the plan or the design) is recorded with it, and where they dispute a finding, that is recorded with their reason.
- **Trial handed over:** a failing case or a test that shows a finding is handed over in a form that can serve as the check for its repair, with the result it gives now.
- **Answer written:** the review's answer to its question, with what was covered and what was not, stands at the top of the record.
- **Worded for the maker:** where the maker is someone else, the findings are drafted for the user in the form in which the maker will get them: about the work, specific, each with its reason.
- **Workspace stated:** the user is told what the review left: nothing, or the trial files and where they are.
- **Back at the same place:** the design, the plan or the build the user came from is taken up where they left it (the step, the open question), and the user is told that this is where they are.
- **Repair on the user's word:** where the user wants a finding repaired at once, that is done after the findings have been stated, as a change of its own, under the rules of the work it belongs to.
- **Next reading named:** where repairs will follow, it is said what a later reading would cover, and what would show each finding settled.
- **Standalone wording:** the written findings have no "as discussed" and no label coined in the review left undefined.
- **Proportion:** the record of a small review is a few lines; a pass with nothing found is a sentence that says what was read.

## Undesirable

### Falls short

- **Findings in the chat:** the findings were said in the conversation and are written nowhere else.
- **Nothing to act on:** "the error handling needs work" names no place and nothing that would settle it.
- **Decisions lost:** which findings the user decided to leave and which they disputed is not recorded, and the next review raises them again.
- **No destination:** a fault of the design is left among the findings on the code, and nobody carries it to the design.
- **Trial not handed over:** the case that showed the fault is gone, and the repair has nothing to be checked against.
- **Left dangling:** the review ends, and the user has to work out where they had left off.
- **Workspace unknown:** the user does not know whether the tree holds trial files.

### Overshoots

- **Repair on the way out:** the findings are repaired, and the code around them tidied, before anybody decided.
- **Report for a glance:** a change of ten lines gets a document with headings, a summary and a table.
- **Unasked documents:** a review file is created in the repository that nobody asked for.
- **Reading diary:** every file opened and every thought on the way is written into the record.
- **Prescribed repair:** each finding dictates its repair down to the line, where the maker needed the fault and what would settle it.
- **Staying on:** the review session carries on into making the repairs and building the next step.

### Counterfeits (looks met, is not)

- **Story as findings:** the course of the reading is told in place of what is wrong.
- **"Addressed":** findings are marked as dealt with, and nothing shows that the fault is gone.
- **Looks standalone:** the sentences are complete, and "the second issue" resolves only with the session.
- **Filed and lost:** the findings are written down, and the user does not know where.
- **Recorded as decided:** a decision stands next to a finding, and the user never made it.
- **Level softened:** a fault of the design is handed back as a small repair in the code, because that is easier to say.
