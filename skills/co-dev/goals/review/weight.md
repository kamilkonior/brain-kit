# Review: Weight

The findings are weighed: each says what it would cost, whom and how often, is marked as a defect, a risk, a question or a preference and carries a grade, what stands in the way of the work going on comes first, and together they answer the review's question.

*Falls short:* a flat list in which the finding that matters is lost. *Overshoots:* every finding pressed as if the work depended on it.

*Borders:* whether a finding is true belongs to proof; what is done about it, and whether the work goes on, belongs to agency; what the review looks for at all belongs to framing; how long the report is and how it is laid out belongs to economy; writing each finding so that it can be acted on, and saying where its fault belongs, belongs to return.

## Desirable

- **Cost named:** each defect and each risk says what it would cost if it were left: what fails, for whom, how badly.
- **Frequency named:** each defect and each risk says how often its case will arise here: on every run, on rare input, only if something else goes wrong as well.
- **Cost now and later:** where it bears on the decision, a finding says what it costs to repair now and what it costs to repair after the work has gone on.
- **Kinds apart:** each finding is marked as what it is: a defect (the work does not do what the standard asks), a risk (nothing is wrong yet, and something may go wrong in a case that is named), a question (you cannot tell) or a preference (you would do it otherwise, and nothing goes wrong).
- **Question and preference weighed:** a question says what depends on its answer, and a preference what would be gained by it.
- **Words for weight:** the grades are few (in the way, worth settling, small), each means one thing throughout, and every finding carries one.
- **Measured by what waits:** a finding's grade follows what the work goes on to: the same gap is in the way of a release and small in a sketch.
- **Heaviest first:** the findings are given in order of weight, not in the order of the files or of the reading.
- **One fault, one finding:** a fault that shows in many places is one finding with its places listed, and is weighed as one.
- **Findings that hang together:** where settling one finding makes others moot (a wrong approach, and the findings on its details), that is said, and the others are not graded on their own.
- **Preferences few and marked:** a preference is given only where it would clearly help, is marked as one, and is never graded above small.
- **Answer to the question:** the review ends in an answer to the frame's question (the work can go on, can go on once these are settled, should not go on as it is), which names the findings it rests on.
- **Answer follows the findings:** the answer is no better and no worse than the findings: no "can go on" over a finding graded as in the way.
- **Regraded in the open:** when a fact or an argument of the user's changes a finding's weight, the grade is changed and the reason is said.

## Undesirable

### Falls short

- **Flat list:** thirty findings come in the order of the files, and the one that loses data is the nineteenth.
- **No cost:** a finding says what is wrong and not what it would cost.
- **Graded down:** a defect that loses data is graded small because its case seems rare, and nobody asked how rare.
- **Kinds mixed:** taste, questions and defects stand in one list with nothing to tell them apart.
- **No answer:** the review ends with the list, and whether the work can go on is left for the reader to work out.
- **Instances scattered:** the same fault is reported twelve times, once per place, and looks like twelve faults.
- **Weight by effort:** the finding that took longest to find is put first.

### Overshoots

- **All in the way:** every finding is graded as in the way.
- **Alarm:** a small risk is told in the words of a disaster.
- **Preference pressed:** a matter of taste is argued as if something broke.
- **Rare made large:** a case that needs three things to go wrong at once is graded by its worst outcome alone.
- **Grading machinery:** scores, matrices and a taxonomy of severities are applied to five findings.
- **Perfect or nothing:** the work is held back for what could be settled after it has gone on, at no extra cost.

### Counterfeits (looks met, is not)

- **Grade without reason:** "critical" is attached, and no cost is named.
- **Inflated to be heard:** a finding is graded up because lower ones were passed over last time.
- **Preference in disguise:** "this is error-prone" or "this is hard to maintain" is said of what is only unfamiliar.
- **Answer from the count:** "only three findings" is offered as a reason that the work is sound, whatever the three are.
- **Borrowed severity:** a fault is graded by the name of its class ("injection", "race"), not by what it would do here.
- **Hedged answer:** "looks mostly fine, but worth a careful look" commits to nothing.
