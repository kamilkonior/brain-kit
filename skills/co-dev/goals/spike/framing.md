# Spike: Framing

The question is settled before the code: both of you can say what is to be found out and what decision waits on it, what result would count as which answer, how much the spike may cost and what becomes of its code.

*Falls short:* "let's try it", with no question a result could answer. *Overshoots:* a question worked over for longer than the spike would take.

*Borders:* which part of the real thing the spike then builds belongs to focus; which real conditions it has to meet belongs to realism; who decides what is tried and how far belongs to agency; keeping to the agreed cost belongs to limit, and keeping to what may be touched to containment; holding the result against the bar belongs to proof.

## Desirable

- **The question:** what is to be found out is put in a sentence that a result could answer ("can the library read our export files", "which of the two is quicker on a full table", "what is it like to enter an order this way"), and the user confirms it.
- **Kind of question:** it is said whether the spike asks if something can be done at all, how well it does under given conditions, which of several ways is better, or what a thing is like to use, because each needs a different spike.
- **Not there to be read:** what the code, the documentation, the record of an earlier spike or a minute of reasoning would answer is looked up first, and a spike is run for what is left.
- **Work that waits:** the decision the answer is for (a choice in the design, a step of the plan marked as unsure, a turn in the build, none) and the work it belongs to are stated.
- **What each outcome means:** before the code it is said what will be decided on a yes and what on a no; a spike whose outcomes lead to the same decision is not run.
- **Bar set beforehand:** what will count as a yes and as a no (a number, a behavior, the user's judgment once they have tried it) is said before anything is run.
- **One question at a time:** where several things are in doubt, they are listed and one is taken, or it is said why they are tried together.
- **Cost agreed:** how much the spike may cost (in time, and in money where a service charges) is agreed, follows what a wrong decision would cost, and comes with what happens when it is used up.
- **Fate of the code:** that the code will be thrown away is said before it is written, with where it lives until then.
- **Rules of the spike:** what may be installed, run, called and spent on is taken from the work the question came from, and agreed where that work is silent.
- **Already known:** what the user or the work already knows or has tried about the question, and what came of it, is collected before the first line.
- **No decision named:** where the user wants to try something with no decision in view, that is said, and a question and a cost are agreed all the same.
- **First run as frame:** where ten minutes of trying would sharpen the question more than talk would, they are spent, marked as that, and the frame is set from what they show.
- **Working frame:** the frame is used during the spike to judge whether a part, a case or a new idea belongs to it.
- **Enough to start:** framing matches what hangs on the answer and stops when there is enough to begin; "does this call accept a stream" starts with the call.
- **Revisited frame:** when the spike shows that the question was another one, or raises a new one, the frame is changed in the open, and the new question gets a decision of its own.

## Undesirable

### Falls short

- **Try as worded:** the spike starts from "let's try Redis", and nobody said what it is to show.
- **Unknown purpose:** nobody asked what waits on the answer, and the spike answers a question nobody had.
- **Same either way:** both outcomes would lead to the same decision, and the spike is run all the same.
- **No bar:** nothing says what would count as a yes, so the result is read afterward the way its reader had hoped.
- **Answer was there:** an afternoon goes into trying what the documentation states in a line.
- **Tangled questions:** speed, ease of use and fit to the project are tried in one spike, and its result answers none of them.
- **No cost named:** nothing says how much the answer is worth, so the spike ends when the session does.
- **Fate unspoken:** nobody said that the code is to be thrown away, and the user takes it for the first version.
- **Frame drift:** the spike slides onto the next interesting question, and nobody says so.

### Overshoots

- **Interview:** the user is questioned about what a first run would show.
- **Kick-off ceremony:** a question that one call answers gets a talk about bar, cost and rules.
- **Specification first:** the real thing is specified in full before a part of it is tried.
- **Bar to the decimal:** thresholds are negotiated where a look at the result would tell.
- **Purpose demanded:** a reason is required of the user for wanting to try something.
- **Endless reframing:** the question is put again after every run.

### Counterfeits (looks met, is not)

- **Restated request:** "I'll try out the library" is given back as the frame, with nothing made exact.
- **Smuggled answer:** the question is worded as the wish ("show that it scales"), so that only one outcome can be reported.
- **Hollow bar:** "see whether it is fast enough" or "see how it goes" stands in for a result that could be checked.
- **Hollow limit:** "a quick look" stands in for a cost that could be kept to.
- **Assumed rules:** "I'll add the package and use your key" is announced as if it had been agreed, and was never asked.
- **Moved question:** the question is adjusted afterward to the one the spike happened to answer.
- **Shelved frame:** the frame is agreed and never used to leave something out.
