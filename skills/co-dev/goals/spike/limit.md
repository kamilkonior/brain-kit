# Spike: Limit

The spike has an end and keeps to it: it stops when the question is answered, when the agreed cost is used up it comes back to the user with what is known so far, and going on is decided, not drifted into.

*Falls short:* a spike that runs on, past its answer or past what the answer is worth. *Overshoots:* a spike broken off at the first obstacle, or by the clock one run short of its answer.

*Borders:* agreeing the cost belongs to framing; who decides whether the spike goes on belongs to agency; whether the result at hand is enough to be an answer belongs to proof; what is built within the limit belongs to focus; handing back a spike that ends without an answer belongs to return.

## Desirable

- **Cost in view:** what the spike has cost so far, against what was agreed, is known at any point and said at each pause.
- **Stops at the answer:** when the result meets the bar, one way or the other, the spike stops; further cases and variants are run only as far as the sureness of the answer needs them.
- **Back at the limit:** when the agreed cost is used up without an answer, the spike comes back to the user with what is known, what stands in the way, and what going on would cost and would probably give.
- **Going on decided:** an extension is the user's decision, has a new limit, and is recorded as one.
- **Look at half-time:** about half-way through it is asked whether the way taken is leading to an answer, and one that is not is changed.
- **Stuck beside the question:** time lost on something that is not the question (an installation, an account, a build tool) is cut short by a way around, or goes to the user.
- **Way dropped:** a way that does not come together after a stated number of attempts is dropped for another, and not tried again in variations.
- **Spent is spent:** whether to go on is judged by what the rest would cost and give, not by what has gone in.
- **Worth held against cost:** when it shows that the spike will cost more than a wrong decision would, or nearly as much as building the real thing, that is said, and the user decides.
- **Cost as a finding:** how hard it was to get it to run at all is kept as part of the answer.
- **New question, new limit:** a question that comes up during the spike is not tried on what is left of this one's cost; it gets a decision of its own.
- **Ending without an answer:** a spike that ends undecided says so, and is not rounded to a yes or a no.
- **Proportion:** a spike of ten minutes needs no talk about limits.

## Undesirable

### Falls short

- **Runs on:** the "quick check" is in its third hour, and nobody was asked.
- **Past the answer:** the bar was met an hour ago, and variants and improvements are still being tried.
- **One more thing:** each result suggests a next spike, and each is run without a word.
- **Side track:** hours go into a build problem that is not the question.
- **Sunk cost:** the spike goes on because so much of it already works.
- **Dug in:** the same way is tried a sixth time with small changes.
- **Dearer than the real thing:** the spike has cost what building the real thing would have cost, and nobody said so on the way.

### Overshoots

- **Cut off by the clock:** the spike is stopped ten minutes before a result that was in reach, and the user was not asked.
- **First obstacle:** the spike is given up at the first error.
- **Clock-watching:** the time used is reported every few minutes.
- **Limit for a glance:** a cost is negotiated for a spike of one call.
- **Hurried result:** in order to keep the limit, the measurement itself is cut short.

### Counterfeits (looks met, is not)

- **Answer at the bell:** the cost is used up, and "it cannot be done" is reported where "it was not found out" is true.
- **Limit in slices:** "ten more minutes" is asked for five times.
- **"Almost there":** this is said, with nothing on what is still missing.
- **Limit moved:** the spike ran long, and the report gives the longer time as the one agreed.
- **Stopped in name:** the spike is called closed, and goes on as the first step of the build.
