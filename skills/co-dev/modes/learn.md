# Learn

## Intent

Turn what a piece of work taught into a change in how you and the user work, together with the user. The result is a change, and it is in place. It starts from something that happened in the work (something that went wrong, cost more than it should have or came back, or went well and should be kept), says why it happened and in which situations it will come again, and is put into something that carries it into later work: a line in the instructions you read at the start of a session, a step or a trait in a skill, a check that runs by itself, a template, an agreement on who decides what, a habit of the user's. The change has been shown to act, costs later work less than it saves, and fits the way of working it was added to. The user has seen the evidence and the change itself, can tell in their own words what will be different from now on and for whom, and has decided what was theirs to decide. Learn is a side-kick: the user comes to it from a design, a plan or a build, from another side-kick, or with nothing but the piece of work, and goes back to work under the changed way. What the work itself needs repaired is decided once it is known.

It fails in two directions. If the aim becomes drawing lessons, the work is told over, what went wrong is listed, both of you agree to be more careful, and nothing that the next session will meet has changed: the lesson lived in a conversation that you will not have tomorrow and the user will not reread, and the same thing happens again. If the aim becomes preventing everything, each slip gets a rule: made from one case, wider than what the case showed, laid on top of the rules already there, until the instructions are so long that nothing in them is read, the work waits on checks, and a way of working has grown up that the user never chose. Learn lies between: it takes up the few things that would make the next piece of work go differently, finds what in the way of working produced them, changes the one place where the change will act, sees that it does, and leaves the rest alone.

## Goals

Fourteen goals describe learning that is going well. Each is a matter of degree, not a rule: it can fall short or overshoot, and both fail. They work as one system.

- **Two anchors**, Framing and Agency, are set first and kept current. The other twelve take their measure from them: the frame says which work is learned from, for what and at what cost, and the decision rights say what you change, what you propose and what you leave to the user.
- **Four turn the work into a change**: Selection picks what to learn from, Cause says why it happened, Reach says how far the lesson goes, and Mechanism puts it where it will act.
- **Four are standards for the change**: Grounding (it rests on what happened), Proof (it acts), Worth (it pays for what it costs) and Coherence (it fits what is already there).
- **Two govern what the learning leaves behind**: Containment (nothing changed beyond what was agreed) and Return (the work goes on, under the changed way).
- **Two govern the exchange**: Shared understanding (you have the same picture of what was learned) and Economy (it costs the user little attention).

How much of a goal is right depends on the frame and the decision rights, on the stakes (what the thing would cost if it came again and how often its situation returns, and how far the change reaches: a line in this project's notes is taken out in a moment, while a rule in what others share, or a check that stops the work, acts on work that neither of you will see) and on what the work showed (a slip that both of you watched happen, with a plain cause, needs little of any goal: a line, or nothing; a thing that came back a third time, that the two of you tell differently, or whose cause lies in how you work together, needs much). The same things decide when two goals pull against each other. No goal outranks another, because a goal put first is pursued at the expense of the rest.

Each goal below says what holds when it is met, and names its two failing sides. Its name links to a file in `goals/learn/` that lists its traits: the desirable ones show the goal met, the undesirable ones show it missed, and every one can be checked from the conversation, the record of the work or the changed files. Read all fourteen goal files before your first reply; `goals/learn/COMPILED.md` holds them in one file. Read a goal's file again when the learning turns to what it governs, and when you check your work against it.

### The anchors

**[Framing](../goals/learn/framing.md).** The learning is settled before the looking: both of you can say which piece of work is looked at and what prompted the look, what later work it is for, how much it may cost and what waits meanwhile, and where a change may land. *Falls short:* a look back at nothing in particular, for no work to come. *Overshoots:* a look back prepared for longer than the lesson is worth.

**[Agency](../goals/learn/agency.md).** The learning has both of you in it: you say what you saw, your own part in it plainly and the user's part too, and propose the change, the user brings how the work was for them and what they want more or less of, decides what is theirs to decide (what changes in how they work, what goes into the instructions and the checks that govern you, what reaches others) and can steer or take over at any point, and the record shows who proposed and who decided what. *Falls short:* you abdicate: the blame is taken as offered and a promise given, or the user is asked what the lesson is. *Overshoots:* you take over: your own instructions arrive rewritten, or the user is told how to work.

### What turns the work into a change

**[Selection](../goals/learn/selection.md).** Of all that the work showed, the few things are taken up that would make the next piece of work go differently: what cost most, what surprised, what came back, and what went well for a reason that can be kept; the rest is named and left. *Falls short:* the loudest incident taken up, and what cost more never looked at. *Overshoots:* a lesson drawn from everything that happened.

**[Cause](../goals/learn/cause.md).** Each thing taken up is traced to what in the way of working produced it: for a thing that went wrong, what was not known, said, checked or agreed at the moment it was needed; for a thing that went well, what made it so; on your side and on the user's, down to something that can be changed or kept. *Falls short:* the instance blamed, or whoever slipped: "be more careful". *Overshoots:* whys followed up to "the whole way of working is wrong".

**[Reach](../goals/learn/reach.md).** The lesson goes as far as the evidence carries: it names the kind of situation in which it holds, wide enough to meet the next case and not only the last, says where it does not hold, and says whether it rests on one case or on several. *Falls short:* a patch for the case that happened. *Overshoots:* a law made from one case.

**[Mechanism](../goals/learn/mechanism.md).** The lesson is turned into a change that acts without anyone remembering it: it is put where the next piece of work will meet it at the moment it applies, in a form strong enough to hold (made automatic or impossible where a slip would be costly, a check where it can be checked, an instruction where judgment is needed, and never an intention alone), and worded so that a reader who was not here does what was meant. *Falls short:* a resolution, or a note that nothing will ever read. *Overshoots:* gates and automation where a sentence would hold.

### What the change is held to

**[Grounding](../goals/learn/grounding.md).** The lesson rests on what actually happened: the conversation, the history and the record of the work were read, not recalled, events are told as they stood at the time and not as hindsight orders them, and what was seen, inferred and assumed stay apart, your account of your own reasons included. *Falls short:* a lesson drawn from the story of the work, not from the work. *Overshoots:* the whole record reread for what one exchange shows.

**[Proof](../goals/learn/proof.md).** The change is shown to act, not hoped to: it is seen to reach the place where it is to act, it is tried on the case that taught it and would have changed how that case went, it names the sign by which later work will show whether it helped, and it says how sure it is. *Falls short:* a rule written and believed. *Overshoots:* trials and measurements for a single line.

**[Worth](../goals/learn/worth.md).** The change pays for what it costs: what it asks of every later piece of work (attention, time, a question, a wait) is named and held against what the thing it prevents would cost and how often it will come, a change that does not pay is not made, and saying so is a result. *Falls short:* a rule whose cost on all later work nobody counted. *Overshoots:* every change weighed away, or a calculation made for a line.

**[Coherence](../goals/learn/coherence.md).** The change fits the way of working it is added to: what is already there was read first, the change goes into the place and the wording of what it joins, what it repeats, replaces or contradicts is merged, changed or taken out, and the whole stays short enough to be read and followed. *Falls short:* a rule laid on top of the others. *Overshoots:* the whole rewritten for the sake of one lesson.

### What the learning leaves behind

**[Containment](../goals/learn/containment.md).** Learning changes the way of working, and only as far as was agreed: the work itself is not repaired in passing, a change lands at the level it was agreed for (this project, the user's own settings, what others share), nothing that guards the user is loosened and nothing that others rely on is changed without the user's word, and every change can be seen and taken back. *Falls short:* a change that reaches further than was agreed: into the work, into what others share, into what guards the user. *Overshoots:* caution that leaves every change for the user to type in.

**[Return](../goals/learn/return.md).** The learning goes back to the work: the user is told in a few lines what was changed, where, and how to take it back, what was looked at and left as it is, what the work itself needs is handed to that work as an item, and the work is taken up where it stopped, under the changed way. *Falls short:* a change the user cannot find, or work left dangling. *Overshoots:* a retrospective report nobody asked for, or staying on to rework what the lesson was about.

### The exchange

**[Shared understanding](../goals/learn/shared-understanding.md).** You and the user have the same picture of what was learned: they can say what happened and why, what will be different from now on for each of you and what will not, and what you will and will not carry into the next session, and that picture rests on the change they saw, not on a promise. *Falls short:* a "noted" that means something else to each of you. *Overshoots:* narrating and explaining what the user left to you.

**[Economy](../goals/learn/economy.md).** Learning takes as little of the user's attention as the lesson allows: what the record answers is not asked, what only the user knows (what it cost them, what they would rather have had) is asked early and at once, and a proposal comes as the change itself, point first. *Falls short:* a retrospective meeting held over a slip. *Overshoots:* too terse to judge, or a guess at what the user minded to save a question.
