# Design

## Intent

Shape what ought to exist, together with the user, while it is still cheap to be wrong. One deliberate design holds — emerging from real alternatives proportional to how open the problem is, standing up under concrete awkwardness and real limits: a clear mental model that serves the purpose, fits the people who will use and live with it, belongs in the system, tames complexity simply, holds up against failure and change, and makes its trade-offs and defaults on purpose. Concrete forms sharpen the problem itself, exposing hidden assumptions, missing ground that belongs in reality first, or unknowns that rest on a cheap trial. Design ends in one coherent whole — what is fixed, what stands as a reopenable default and what stays open to building unmistakable — owned by the user and captured proportionally as a shared reference.

It falls short when an accidental shape stands, candidates pass on abstract plausibility or a happy path, load-bearing trade-offs, seams or unknowns go unexamined, choices stay open or defaults hide in prose, or the shape is not the user's. It overshoots when divergence rituals run in a constrained space, paper settles what only a trial, building or real use can show, task order stands in for an unsettled shape, or weighing goes on after criteria have separated contenders.

#### Brainstorming

Genuinely different ideas have room to take shape — concrete enough to picture, proportional to how open the problem is, and with the user's own hunches among them.

*Falls short:* the first workable or stock idea stands, or half-formed hunches are shot down on arrival. *Overshoots:* ideas pile up past use, or divergence rituals run where constraints already narrow the field.

Why: the first idea that works is rarely the soundest and costs the most to replace once the design rests on it, while filler options waste attention.

**Desirable**

- **Diverge, then converge:** ideas have room to form before they are judged, rather than being shot down as they appear.
- **Brainwriting:** the user's own hunches and ideas have room to surface before the agent's proposals anchor the space.
- **Yes, and:** a half-formed idea — especially the user's — reaches its strongest form before it is tested.
- **Lateral thinking:** ideas differ in kind — who acts, when, where, or what is left out — not only in settings.
- **Prior art:** how matching forces were met elsewhere is weighed on fit, not copied blindly.
- **Thumbnail sketch:** each idea is concrete enough to picture and compare at the same level, cheap to bend or discard.

**Undesirable**

- **Premature convergence:** the first workable idea stands, or ideas are judged before they take shape.
- **Yes, but:** a half-formed idea is met with objections before its strongest form is seen.
- **Variations on a theme:** one idea with different settings is presented as several.
- **The usual suspects:** only the approaches anyone would think of first appear.
- **Spray and pray:** ideas pile up in volume with nothing showing which stand out.
- **Workshop theater:** divergence rituals or filler options stand where constraints or a clear hypothesis already narrow the field.

#### Reality check

Candidate shapes hold up against concrete awkwardness, human ergonomics, real limits, and rough numbers — and what reasoning cannot settle rests on a live trial.

*Falls short:* a shape passes on abstract plausibility or a happy path, ignores real limits, or gambles a load-bearing unknown on paper. *Overshoots:* contrived hypotheticals stall a sound shape, or paper chases certainty that only building and real use can give.

Why: flaws are cheapest while the shape is still ink, while settling empirical unknowns by debate builds confidence that breaks on first contact with reality.

**Desirable**

- **Desk check:** the shape holds up under concrete normal, awkward, failing and boundary cases, exposing broken invariants and vague seams before anything is built.
- **Paper prototype:** the shape is tried in concrete form from the side of the people who will use, call, and live with it, not only as an abstract structure.
- **Back-of-the-envelope calculation:** where a limit is a number, such as load, size or cost, a rough figure shows whether the shape stays within it.
- **Pre-mortem:** the shape is viewed as if it had already failed in building or use, surfacing hidden seams, missing ground, or bad assumptions while changing it is cheap.
- **Acid test:** a load-bearing unknown that reasoning and inspection cannot settle waits on a cheap, disposable trial rather than on argument.

**Undesirable**

- **Boxes and arrows:** candidates stay so abstract that seams, data shapes, and lived flow look tidy only because nothing concrete touched them.
- **Blue-sky thinking:** a candidate breaks a stated limit or system reality with nothing saying what would have to give.
- **Grading on a curve:** a shape is chosen because it beat weaker contenders without holding up against the real need and limits.
- **Paper gamble:** a load-bearing unknown rests on argument alone though only reality or a cheap trial could settle it.

#### Convergence

Open choices close against criteria stated first, turning on what truly separates contenders into one deliberate direction.

*Falls short:* choices stay open, or contenders blur into an incoherent compromise. *Overshoots:* criteria are rigged after the fact, or weighing continues after criteria have separated contenders.

Why: a choice nobody makes here still gets made later by accident, while chasing certainty past clear separation wastes attention.

**Desirable**

- **Decision criteria:** what contenders are judged by stands clear before they are weighed.
- **Crux:** comparison turns on the few points where contenders genuinely differ in consequence, not on everything that could be said of them.
- **Kill your darlings:** attachment to an early sketch or pet shape yields when criteria or reality favour a cleaner one.
- **Tripwire:** a high-stakes decision resting on an uncertain assumption names what would reopen it.

**Undesirable**

- **Sitting on the fence:** "it depends", or an unresolved list of pros and cons, stands where a choice was due.
- **Analysis paralysis:** weighing goes on after the criteria have separated the contenders.
- **Texas sharpshooter:** the criteria are chosen or reweighted after the fact to fit the option already preferred.
- **Design by committee:** a blend is made in order to close, keeping no option's strengths.

#### Commitment

Settled choices stand together as one coherent, user-owned design whose fixed commitments, reopenable defaults, and open room for building are unmistakable.

*Falls short:* load-bearing choices hide as silent defaults in prose, or parts are approved without confirming the whole. *Overshoots:* reversible details or internal code are fixed on paper, or task order stands in for an unsettled shape.

Why: later work can adapt safely only when what is fixed stands clearly apart from what is default or open, while over-specifying rots on first contact with reality.

**Desirable**

- **One-way vs. two-way doors:** hard-to-reverse commitments are locked by the user here, while cheap-to-reverse choices stand as reopenable defaults or stay open to building.
- **Last responsible moment:** a choice that can wait at no cost waits for what will inform it, and no longer.
- **Sign-off:** settled choices stand together as one coherent design, its boundaries and trade-offs in view, confirmed as a whole by the user.

**Undesirable**

- **Buried default:** a consequential choice sits unspoken inside prose or an example, neither chosen by the user nor marked as a reopenable default.
- **Big design up front:** the design fixes on paper what only building and real use can show.
- **Premature sequencing:** the design is a sequence of tasks before its shape, boundaries and trade-offs hold, locking in an unexamined form.
