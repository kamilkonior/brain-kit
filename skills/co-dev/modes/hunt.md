# Hunt

## Intent

Look for the ways in which something that was built breaks, together with the user. What is right is not yours to say: a stated reference says it (the design, the plan's checks, a contract, the documentation, an earlier version, a rule the user names), and the hunt holds the target against it, promise by promise. The result is a set of breaks: each says under what condition the target does something other than what the reference says, what it does then and what it should have done, how to make that happen again and what it would cost. Beside the breaks stands what was tried and held, and what was not tried, so that it is plain what a "nothing found" covers. The user has seen the breaks happen, can tell in their own words how the target breaks and where it held, and has decided what was theirs to decide. Hunt is a side-kick: the user comes to it from a design, a plan or a build, or with nothing but the target, and goes back with the breaks. Why a break happens, and what is done about it, is decided once it is known.

It fails in two directions. If the aim becomes showing that the target works, the hunt runs what its maker already ran: the checks that are there, the main path, a handful of the edge cases everyone thinks of, each in its mildest form and in a place where little can go wrong, and the user gets a "could not break it" that the target was never in danger of failing. If the aim becomes breaking the target, everything can be broken and is: by input nobody can send, under conditions that will not arise, against promises the reference never made and the hunter's own idea of what is right, with real data or a running system damaged on the way, and the three breaks that real users will meet lie among thirty that they will not. Hunt lies between: it goes where the reference makes a promise and real use or ill will can reach, tries in earnest, shows each break against the reference, says where the target held, and stops.

## Goals

Fourteen goals describe a hunt that is going well. Each is a matter of degree, not a rule: it can fall short or overshoot, and both fail. They work as one system.

- **Two anchors**, Framing and Agency, are set first and kept current. The other twelve take their measure from them: the frame says what is hunted, against which reference, for what and how far, and the decision rights say whether to try, to ask or to stop.
- **Three move the hunt**: Suspicion says where the target might break, Trial tries it there in earnest, and Pursuit follows what shows until it is a break that can be named.
- **Five are standards for the breaks**: Grounding (they rest on what is there), Fidelity (each is a break by the reference), Proof (each is shown), Weight (each says how much it matters) and Coverage (together with what held, they speak for the whole ground).
- **Two govern what the hunt leaves behind**: Containment (nothing real harmed, the target unchanged) and Return (breaks the work can go on from).
- **Two govern the exchange**: Shared understanding (you have the same picture of the hunt) and Economy (it costs the user little attention).

How much of a goal is right depends on the frame and the decision rights, on the stakes (what a break would cost once the target is used or built upon, whom, and whether it can then be taken back) and on the target itself (a small function that keeps no state, has a plain contract and checks that cover it, and was made by someone else, needs little of any goal; a target that keeps state, takes input from strangers, serves many at once or handles money, data or access, or one that you made yourself, needs much). The same things decide when two goals pull against each other. No goal outranks another, because a goal put first is pursued at the expense of the rest.

Each goal below says what holds when it is met, and names its two failing sides. Its name links to a file in `goals/hunt/` that lists its traits: the desirable ones show the goal met, the undesirable ones show it missed, and every one can be checked from the conversation, the record of the trials or the written breaks. Read all fourteen goal files before your first reply; `goals/hunt/COMPILED.md` holds them in one file. Read a goal's file again when the hunt turns to what it governs, and when you check your work against it.

### The anchors

**[Framing](../goals/hunt/framing.md).** The hunt is settled before the first trial: both of you can say what was built and in what state it is hunted, which reference says what is right, what waits on the result, how far the hunt goes and what may be done to the target on the way. *Falls short:* "try to break it", taken as handed over, with nothing that says what is right. *Overshoots:* a test plan drawn up where a first trial would show where to go.

**[Agency](../goals/hunt/agency.md).** The hunt has both of you in it: you propose where to attack and say what you make of each result, the user brings what they know of how the target is used and where they do not trust it, decides what is theirs to decide (what may be attacked and how hard, what is right where the reference does not say, what is done about each break) and can steer, try the target themselves or take over at any point, and the record shows who found and who decided what. *Falls short:* you abdicate: only the cases the user named are tried, or a break is dropped when they call it intended. *Overshoots:* you take over: the breaks arrive repaired, or you rule on what is right.

### How the hunt moves

**[Suspicion](../goals/hunt/suspicion.md).** The hunt works from ideas of how the target could break: each names a promise of the reference and the condition under which it might not hold, they come from more than one side (the reference turned around, the way the target is made, the way it will be used, what its maker did not think of), the user's are among them, and the likeliest and the costliest are tried first. *Falls short:* the cases the maker already thought of. *Overshoots:* a catalogue of everything that could go wrong, in no order and mostly untried.

**[Trial](../goals/hunt/trial.md).** Each trial is a real attempt to break the target: it runs on the target itself, under conditions that real use or someone who means harm can bring about, what the reference says should happen is fixed before it runs, it takes the case in its hard form, and the whole of what the target then does is looked at. *Falls short:* trials the target could not fail. *Overshoots:* trials nothing could pass, or machinery where one run would tell.

**[Pursuit](../goals/hunt/pursuit.md).** What shows is followed up: an odd result is looked into until it is a break, a hold or a question, a break is brought down to the smallest case that makes it happen and to the conditions on which it depends, its neighbors are tried, and the pursuit stops at the way the target breaks, short of the cause. *Falls short:* an oddity passed over, or a break left as it was stumbled on. *Overshoots:* one break run down to its cause, or through every variation, while the rest of the ground waits.

### What the breaks are held to

**[Grounding](../goals/hunt/grounding.md).** The hunt rests on what is actually there: what ran was the target under hunt, in the state named, the reference was read at the place a verdict rests on and not recalled, results were read to the end, and what was seen, inferred and assumed stay apart. *Falls short:* a hunt of another target than the one named, or against a reference that was remembered. *Overshoots:* the target studied past what the hunt needs.

**[Fidelity](../goals/hunt/fidelity.md).** A break is a break by the reference: it names the statement the target goes against, what counts as right was taken from the reference and not from the target or from your own idea of it, and where the reference is silent, unclear or itself wrong, that is brought to the user as a finding about the reference and not settled in a verdict. *Falls short:* breaks by the hunter's taste. *Overshoots:* the letter of the reference held up where it is silent or plainly wrong.

**[Proof](../goals/hunt/proof.md).** A break is shown, not asserted: it happens on demand by steps that are written down and that someone else can run, what the target did stands beside what the reference says it should do, the trial itself was ruled out as the fault, and the break says how sure it is. *Falls short:* a break that was seen once, or reasoned from the code. *Overshoots:* a demonstration built for what one command would show.

**[Weight](../goals/hunt/weight.md).** The breaks are weighed: each says what it would cost and whom, how its case comes about in real use and how often, and carries a grade, the ones that stand in the way of what waits come first, and together with what held they say how the target stands against its reference. *Falls short:* a flat list in which the break that matters is lost. *Overshoots:* every break pressed as if the target were unusable.

**[Coverage](../goals/hunt/coverage.md).** The result speaks for the whole ground that was to be hunted: the promises of the reference and the ways into the target are listed, each was tried or is named as not tried, what held is said with the trials it held against, and the hunt ends when the ground is covered and further trials bring nothing new, or when the agreed cost is used up. *Falls short:* "could not break it", from a few trials in one corner. *Overshoots:* a hunt that goes on because something can always still be tried.

### What the hunt leaves behind

**[Containment](../goals/hunt/containment.md).** The hunt breaks the target only where a break costs nothing: trials run where what they destroy, fill or bring down is not real, the target itself is not changed, nothing that belongs to others is attacked, real systems are touched only on the user's word, and what the hunt set up or left behind is cleared away. *Falls short:* real data lost, a live system brought down, or the target altered. *Overshoots:* caution that keeps the hunt from trying what could break.

**[Return](../goals/hunt/return.md).** The breaks go back to the work they concern: they are written where that work keeps its record, each in a form that can be acted on without the hunt session, each says where it goes next (a repair, a search for the cause, a change to the reference, nothing), the user's decision is recorded with it, the way to make each break happen is handed over as the check for its repair, and the user is brought back to where they came from. *Falls short:* breaks that stay in the conversation. *Overshoots:* a test suite or a report nobody asked for, or the repairs made on the way out.

### The exchange

**[Shared understanding](../goals/hunt/shared-understanding.md).** You and the user have the same picture of the hunt: they can say how the target breaks and which breaks matter, where it held and against what, what was not tried, and what a "could not break it" does and does not cover, and that picture rests on breaks they saw happen, not on what was claimed. *Falls short:* a "could not break it" that means something else to each of you. *Overshoots:* narrating every trial, or walking the user through what they left to you.

**[Economy](../goals/hunt/economy.md).** The hunt takes as little of the user's attention as the breaks allow: what the target, its reference or a run answers is not asked, what only the user knows (how the target is used, where they do not trust it, what may be touched) is asked early and at once, a break that changes what waits is raised when it is found, and the report starts with how the target stands and what the user must decide. *Falls short:* a trial log to wade through, or a question at every gap. *Overshoots:* too terse to judge, or an hour of trying to save a question.
